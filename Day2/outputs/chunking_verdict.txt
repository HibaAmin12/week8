CHUNKING STRATEGIES — FINAL VERDICTS
====================================

Use Case:
RAG-based text processing

Experiment:
The same cleaned dataset (3,033 words) was used as input for all
five chunking strategies. Only the chunking method was changed.


============================================================
1. FIXED-SIZE CHUNKING
============================================================

Configuration:
- Chunk size: 500 words
- Overlap: 50 words
- Total chunks: 7
- Average chunk size: 477 words
- Minimum: 333 words
- Maximum: 501 words

Verdict:
Fixed-size chunking is simple, predictable, and easy to control.

Main Issues:
- It does not consider sentence or topic boundaries.
- It can split a sentence or idea between two chunks.
- It may place different topics inside the same chunk.
- Large chunks can contain unnecessary information.

Overall:
Good for simple applications where consistent chunk size is the
main requirement, but it does not preserve natural text structure well.


============================================================
2. SENTENCE CHUNKING
============================================================

Configuration:
- 5 sentences per chunk
- Total chunks: 31
- Average chunk size: 98.81 words
- Minimum: 34 words
- Maximum: 224 words

Verdict:
Sentence chunking preserves sentence boundaries and produces
relatively focused chunks.

Main Issues:
- Chunk sizes are not consistent.
- Some sentences are much longer than others.
- The current simple sentence splitter can incorrectly split
  abbreviations such as "St. Joseph".

Overall:
A good approach when sentence-level context is important, but
its effectiveness depends on accurate sentence detection.


============================================================
3. PARAGRAPH CHUNKING
============================================================

Configuration:
- One paragraph per chunk
- Total chunks: 20
- Average chunk size: 152.60 words
- Minimum: 84 words
- Maximum: 250 words

Verdict:
Paragraph chunking preserves the natural structure of the dataset
very well. Each chunk generally represents a coherent topic.

Main Issues:
- Chunk size cannot be controlled directly.
- Large paragraphs can create unnecessarily large chunks.
- Very small paragraphs may provide insufficient context.
- It depends heavily on the quality of the original document structure.

Overall:
A simple and context-preserving strategy that works well when the
source document already has meaningful paragraph boundaries.


============================================================
4. SEMANTIC CHUNKING
============================================================

Configuration:
- Embedding model: all-MiniLM-L6-v2
- Similarity threshold: 0.50
- Total chunks: 126
- Average chunk size: 25.06 words
- Minimum: 2 words
- Maximum: 112 words

Verdict:
Semantic chunking attempts to divide the text according to
meaning rather than only position or formatting.

Main Issues:
- The current implementation produced too many chunks.
- The average chunk size is only 25 words.
- Some chunks contain as little as 2 words.
- Very small chunks can lose surrounding context.
- It requires embedding computation, making it more computationally
  expensive.
- The result depends strongly on the similarity threshold.

Overall:
Conceptually powerful, but the current configuration is too aggressive
for this dataset. It would need tuning and a minimum chunk-size rule
before being considered a practical choice.


============================================================
5. RECURSIVE CHUNKING
============================================================

Configuration:
- Target chunk size: 500 words
- Overlap: 50 words
- Total chunks: 7
- Average chunk size: 434.14 words
- Minimum: 346 words
- Maximum: 498 words

Hierarchy:
Paragraph → Sentence → Word

Verdict:
Recursive chunking attempts to preserve natural boundaries while
also maintaining a controlled chunk size.

Main Issues:
- The current implementation can combine multiple paragraphs into
  one chunk.
- Therefore, different topics can sometimes appear in the same chunk.
- Overlap introduces some duplicated information.
- Its quality depends on the separators and sentence-splitting logic.

Overall:
It provides a good balance between natural text boundaries and
controlled chunk size.


============================================================
FINAL COMPARISON
============================================================

FIXED-SIZE
→ Simple and predictable, but can break sentences and concepts.

SENTENCE
→ Preserves sentence boundaries, but chunk sizes vary.

PARAGRAPH
→ Preserves natural document structure, but chunk size is uncontrolled.

SEMANTIC
→ Meaning-based and flexible, but currently creates chunks that are
  too small and requires more computation.

RECURSIVE
→ Balances natural boundaries with controlled chunk size.


============================================================
FINAL RECOMMENDATION
============================================================

Recommended Strategy: RECURSIVE CHUNKING

Based on the chunking experiment performed on the same dataset,
Recursive Chunking provides the most practical balance.

It combines hierarchical text boundaries with a target chunk size.
Compared with Fixed-size Chunking, it is more aware of the structure
of the text. Compared with Sentence and Paragraph Chunking, it provides
better control over chunk size. Compared with the current Semantic
Chunking implementation, it avoids producing a very large number of
extremely small chunks.

Therefore, Recursive Chunking is the recommended starting strategy
for this RAG use case.

Important:
This recommendation is based specifically on the current chunking
implementations, their generated chunk sizes, and inspection of the
resulting chunks. Semantic chunking could become more suitable after
proper threshold tuning and minimum chunk-size constraints.
